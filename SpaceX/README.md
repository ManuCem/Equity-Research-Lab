# SpaceX (SPCX): a basic accounting analysis

> **Personal learning project, not investment advice.** Facts, ratios and questions only: no buy/sell view, price target or valuation.

A beginner-level look at SpaceX's accounts, built only from its SEC documents: the S-1/A prospectus (years 2023-2025 and Q1 2026) and the Q2 and Q3 2026 earnings releases. It answers five simple questions: how big is the business, where does the money go, is it profitable, is it generating or using cash, and how much does it owe? It also covers six ratios, a step-by-step bridge from adjusted EBITDA to net income (the challenge) and open questions to investigate.

**Read it:** [`spacex.html`](spacex.html). Once GitHub Pages is enabled for this repo, the page is at
`https://manucem.github.io/Micro-Lab/SpaceX/spacex.html`.

## Main takeaways

- **Fast growth, changing mix.** Revenue went from $10.4bn (2023) to $18.7bn (2025). In 2025 Starlink (Connectivity) was the biggest segment. By Q3 2026 the AI / "Super Intelligence" segment, mainly renting computing power to other companies, was the largest.
- **R&D is the big cost.** Research and development rose from about a fifth of revenue in 2023 to almost half in 2025 (Starship, AI). Costs exceeded revenue in every period shown except Q3 2026.
- **Profit arrived in Q3 2026,** helped by items outside the core business: other income, interest income and a tax benefit. Operating margin turned clearly positive in the same quarter.
- **Cash is being consumed.** Operating cash flow is positive and growing, but capex is several times larger. The June 2026 IPO and new debt fund the gap.
- **The balance sheet changed overnight.** Net debt turned into large net cash after the IPO.

## What is in this folder

```
SpaceX/
├── README.md                   this file
├── spacex.html                 the web page (generated)
├── filings/                    the source documents + SOURCES.md (the transcript stays local, see .gitignore)
├── data/
│   ├── spacex_data.csv         ONE data file: every number with its document and page
│   └── spacex_accounting.xlsx  workbook: statements, ratios (Excel formulas), bridge, checks, sources (generated)
├── scripts/
│   ├── build_outputs.py        reads the CSV and writes the workbook, the page and the key-numbers block above
│   └── verify_sources.py       checks that every number in the CSV is printed on the page it cites
└── lib/echarts.js              chart library, stored locally (no CDN)
```

## How to reproduce

```bash
pip install openpyxl pdfplumber          # pdfplumber is only needed for verify_sources.py
python SpaceX/scripts/verify_sources.py  # optional: every CSV number is on its cited PDF page
python SpaceX/scripts/build_outputs.py   # rebuilds spacex_accounting.xlsx, spacex.html and the README block
```

To fix or add a number, edit `data/spacex_data.csv` (keep the source and page) and run both scripts again. Never edit the generated files by hand.

## How the numbers were checked

1. **Source check:** `verify_sources.py` opens each cited PDF page and confirms that every typed-in number is printed there.
2. **Arithmetic checks:** the workbook's *Checks* tab tests whether segments add up to totals, the balance sheet balances, the cash flow reconciles, quarters add up to year-to-date, and the accumulated deficit moves by net income. Failures are red, with an explanation.

## Open questions and data gaps

Figures that are not in these files are left empty, never estimated. They are listed in `spacex.html` (Data notes) and in the workbook's *Sources* tab. Examples: Q4 2025 on its own; the balance sheets at end-2023 and 30 Sep 2025; Q2 and Q3 cash flows on their own; the notes that would explain Q3 2026 "other income".
